Santhoshraj Srinivasan

Where I grew up, questions belonged to the powerful. So I learned to ask.

I grew up in a rural village in Tamil Nadu, India, watching my blind father fight his way to becoming a teacher. That fight led me to social work, with families in villages, prisons, and HIV programs across India. Today, at the University of Tennessee, I study why parents burn out under long-term stress, share that research at national conferences, and teach future social workers to ask better questions of the people they serve.

Research program

When caring wears parents down.

Raising a child is demanding. When that stress lasts for months and years, some parents reach burnout: exhausted, distant from their children, and no longer the parent they want to be. My dissertation asks how that happens, and what could help. It anchors a wider agenda on trauma in families, prisons, and marginalized communities, shown below.

Parenting demands Parenting stress Parental burnout

Guided by the Balance Between Risks and Resources theory of parental burnout (Mikolajczak & Roskam, 2018): burnout grows when a parent's demands outweigh their resources for too long.

Do parenting stress and burnout go together, and which comes first?
Design
Systematic review (PRISMA 2020)
Evidence
18 reports, 17 independent samples
Status
Completed

Stress and burnout consistently occur together. Only a few studies test which comes first, and none follows stress that lasts for years.

Does parenting stress when a child is 9 shape how parents see themselves, and how close they feel to their child, at 15?
Design
Longitudinal structural equation modeling
Data
Future of Families and Child Wellbeing Study, 3,347 caregivers
Status
In progress

Following the same U.S. families for six years lets me ask whether earlier stress comes before later strain.

Does daily caregiving time relate to burnout differently depending on the age of a parent's youngest child?
Design
Multilevel modeling
Data
International Investigation of Parental Burnout, 17,409 parents in 42 countries
Status
In progress

Comparing parents across countries shows which parts of burnout travel across cultures and which depend on context.

Teaching

Ask. Act. Lead.

Most students expect research to be the course farthest from practice. I teach it as the course that helps them see their clients and communities more clearly.

Ask

Start with a real question

Every student builds a research question from their own field placement, whether in a school, a hospital, or a child welfare office.

Act

Answer it with evidence

They learn design, sampling, and ethics by applying each one to a problem they care about. Who would a convenience sample leave out at your agency?

Lead

Share it and change things

Students take what they learn back to their peers and agencies, and every finding raises a new question.

"You chose social work, but social work
also chose you to lead."

The verse I carry

Thirukkural 393

From Valluvar's chapter on Learning (கல்வி), written about two thousand years ago.

கண்ணுடையர் என்பவர் கற்றோர் முகத்திரண்டு
புண்ணுடையர் கல்லா தவர்.

திருக்குறள் 393 · அதிகாரம் 40, கல்வி

"Those who have learned are the ones who truly have eyes. The unlearned have only two wounds upon the face."

My father cannot see. By Valluvar's measure, he never lacked eyes: he fought for his education, became a teacher, and opened mine. That is why I teach research. I want every student to see the people that systems overlook.
